# French Grammar Explainer

Explain French grammar concepts in depth. Answer "why" questions about French grammar structures, tenses, moods, and patterns.

## Input Types

Process these question types:
- "Why do we use subjunctive here?"
- "When do I use imparfait vs passé composé?"
- "What's the difference between because and although?"
- "Why is this verb in the conditional?"
- Any specific grammar question about a sentence or construction

## Workflow

### 1. Analyze the Grammar Question

Identify:
- **The specific construction** being asked about
- **The grammatical concept** involved (subjunctive, tense, mood, pronoun, etc.)
- **The context** where it appears (formal, spoken, written, etc.)

### 2. Grammar Breakdown

Explain the structure:
- **Form**: How is it constructed?
- **Components**: What elements make up the pattern?
- **Variation**: What are the different forms?

### 3. Tense/Mood Explanation

Cover temporal and modal aspects:
- **When used**: Time relationship (past, present, future)
- **Mood**: Indicative, subjunctive, conditional, imperative
- **Aspect**: Simple vs progressive vs perfect

### 4. Semantic/Emotional Reason

Explain the "why" - the meaning behind the choice:
- **Volition**: Willingness, desire, fear (subjunctive triggers)
- **Uncertainty**: Doubt, possibility, probability
- **Emotion**: Surprise, sadness, joy
- **Evaluation**: Judgement, opinion, necessity
- **Temporal**: After, before, during sequences
- **Contrast**: Despite, although, provided that

### 5. Similar Structures

Show related patterns:
- **Parallels**: Other verbs/expressions with same trigger
- **Alternatives**: Different ways to express same meaning
- **Nuances**: Subtle differences between options

### 6. Common Mistakes

Identify pitfalls:
- **Overuse**: When NOT to use the pattern
- **Underuse**: When the pattern is needed but missed
- **Confusion**: Similar-looking structures with different meanings
- **Transfer errors**: From English or other languages

### 7. Generate Obsidian Graph Links

Create connections to related concepts:
- Link to related grammar notes
- Cross-reference trigger words
- Connect to similar patterns

### Example Output: "Why subjunctive here?"

```markdown
# Grammar: Subjunctive in subordinate clause

## The Sentence
"Il faut que je parte."

## Breakdown
- **Main clause**: Il faut (it's necessary)
- **Subordinate**: que je parte (that I leave)
- **Pattern**: Verb of necessity + que + subjunctive

## Tense
- **Present subjunctive** "parte" = current/ongoing action
- **Past subjunctive** "sois parti" = action completed before main clause

## Why Subjunctive Here?
The subjunctive expresses:
- **Volition**: "It is necessary that I leave" - not a fact, but a requirement
- **Subjectivity**: The main clause expresses will, not observation
- **Incomplete reality**: The action hasn't happened yet, it's a goal

**Key principle**: The subjunctive is used when the subordinate clause describes:
- Something desired (not a fact)
- Something necessary (not certain)
- Something feared/possible (not confirmed)

## Similar Structures

| Expression | Type | Example |
|------------|------|---------|
| Il faut que | necessity | Il faut que tu travailles |
| Il vaut mieux que | better | Il vaut mieux que tu partes |
| C'est важно que | important | C'est important que tu comprends |
| Il est nécessaire que | required | Il est nécessaire qu'elle soit là |

## Common Mistakes

❌ *Il faut que je vais* (incorrect - indicative after "il faut")
✅ *Il faut que j'aille* (subjunctive required)

❌ *Je veux que tu fais* (English interference)
✅ *Je veux que tu fasses* (subjunctive after verbs of wanting)

❌ *C'est bien qu'il vient* (treats as fact)
✅ *C'est bien qu'il vienne* (expresses satisfaction, not certainty)
```

### Example Output: "Imparfait vs Passé Composé"

```markdown
# Grammar: Imparfait vs Passé Composé

## The Context
"Quand j'étais étudiant, je **travaillais** dans un café."

## Breakdown

### Passé Composé
- **Aspect**: Completed action, has a clear end point
- **Usage**: Specific moment, one-time events, result matters
- **Translation**: "I worked" (as a completed event)

### Imparfait
- **Aspect**: Ongoing, habitual, or descriptive
- **Usage**: Background, recurring actions, states of being
- **Translation**: "I was working" or "I used to work"

## Why Imparfait Here?
- **Background setting**: "When I was a student" - describes a period
- **Habitual action**: "I worked" (not a specific event, but regular)
- **Duration**: No specific start/end mentioned

## Decision Tree

```
Is the action...
├── COMPLETED in the past? → Passé Composé
│   "J'ai mangé" (I ate - done)
├── ONGOING or HABITUAL? → Imparfait
│   "Je mangeais" (I was eating / used to eat)
├── DESCRIBING a state? → Imparfait
│   "J'étais fatigué" (I was tired)
├── SPECIFIC moment mentioned? → Passé Composé
│   "Ce matin, j'ai mangé à 7h"
└── BACKGROUND to another event? → Imparfait
    "Je mangeais quand il est arrivé"
```

## Similar Structures

| Context | Tense | Reason |
|---------|-------|--------|
| Story background | Imparfait | Ongoing description |
| Specific event | Passé Composé | One-time action |
| Habit in past | Imparfait | "used to" |
| Result achieved | Passé Composé | Completed |
| Physical description | Imparfait | State |
| Action in progress | Imparfait | Continuous |

## Common Mistakes

❌ *Hier, je mangeais au restaurant* (specific day = completed)
✅ *Hier, j'ai mangé au restaurant*

❌ *Il a été fatigué toute la journée* (state = imparfait)
✅ *Il était fatigué toute la journée*

❌ *Quand je suis allé, il faisait beau* (simultaneous = both imparfait)
✅ *Quand j'y suis allé, il faisait beau*
```
