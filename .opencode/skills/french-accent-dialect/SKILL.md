# French Accent & Dialect Analyzer

Identify and analyze different French accents and dialects. Understand slang, pronunciation variations, and cultural context for authentic immersion.

## Input Types

Process:
- Audio (podcast, video, native speaker)
- Video with French dialogue
- Text (social media, literature, dialogue)
- Transcription with accent markers

## French Dialects Covered

### 🇫🇷 France French (Hexagone)
- Standard Parisian French
- Regional accents (Provence, Breton, Alsatian)
- Social class variations

### 🇨🇦 Québec French (Québéquois)
- Quebec City dialect
- Montreal French
- Regional variations within Quebec

### 🇧🇪 Belgian French (Belge)
- Walloon dialect influence
- Brussels French
- Ardennes region

### 🇸🇳 African French
- West Africa (Senegal, Ivory Coast, Mali)
- North Africa (Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia)
- Central Africa (Congo, Cameroon)
- Indian Ocean (Madagascar, Mauritius)

### 🇨🇭 Swiss French
- Vaud region
- Neuchâtel area

### Other
- Louisiana French
- Caribbean (Haiti, Guadeloupe, Martinique)
- Vietnam French (historical)

## Workflow

### 1. Identify the Accent

Detect from:
- Vocabulary choices
- Pronunciation patterns
- Grammar constructions
- Cultural references
- Interjections and filler words

### 2. Analyze Pronunciation Differences

Key differences between dialects:
- Vowel quality and duration
- Consonant articulation
- Nasal vowel patterns
- Stress and intonation
- Connected speech patterns

### 3. Document Slang & Expressions

Region-specific vocabulary:
- Everyday slang
- Informal expressions
- Swear words and emphasis
- Idioms unique to the region

### 4. Explain Cultural Context

Why this dialect exists:
- Historical influences
- Local culture impact
- Social factors
- Language contact (indigenous languages)

```markdown

## Pronunciation Features

| Feature | France | Québec | Difference |
|---------|--------|--------|------------|
| "oi" | /wa/ | /wɛ/ | More open in Quebec |
| "ou" | /u/ | /ʊ/ ~ /u/ | Slightly centralized |
| "r" | /ʁ/ | /ɾ/ or /r/ | Often alveolar in Quebec |

## Vocabulary Differences

| Standard French | Québec | Meaning |
|-----------------|--------|---------|
| une.auto | un.char | car |
| faire.des.courses | magasiner | to shop |
| très | ben | very |
| maintenant | là | now |

## Slang & Expressions

**Common**:
- "C'est quoi ça ?" - What's that? (instead of "Qu'est-ce que c'est ?")
- "Tabernacle!" - Shoot! (mild swear)
- "Asti" - Damn (swear word)

**Everyday**:
- "checker" - to check (anglicism)
- "flusher" - to flush (anglicism)
- "crisser" - to throw (vulgar)

## Cultural Notes

- Heavy anglicisms from contact with English Canada
- "tu" usage very common (even in formal contexts)
- Food and hockey references common
- "s'a" / "c'est ça" as sentence starters
```

### Complete Regional Comparison

```markdown
# French Dialects Comparison

## 🇫🇷 France French

### Characteristics
- Formal "vous" default in professional contexts
- Standard Parisian pronunciation
- Complex grammar structures in writing
- Clear distinction between formal/informal registers

### Common Slang
- "kiffer" - to love
- "crever" - to be exhausted
- "naze" - stupid
- "grave" - really (very)
- "bouffer" - to eat (informal)
- "barjo" - crazy

### Pronunciation
- Uvular /ʁ/ (guttural r)
- Clear vowel distinctions
- Balanced stress
- Liaison in formal speech

### Cultural Context
- Class-conscious language
- Regional accents seen as markers of origin
- "verlan" (reversed slang) popular in youth culture

---

## 🇨🇦 Québec French

### Characteristics
- "tu" used almost universally (less vous)
- Distinct vocabulary from France
- More direct communication style
- Strong identity through language

### Common Slang (Joual)
- "char" - car
- "magasiner" - to shop
- "tabernacle" - shoot
- "c'est quoi" - what's that
- "ben" - very, really
- "astie" - damn
- "crisser" - to throw/put (vulgar)
- "pantoute" - not at all

### Pronunciation
- More open /ɛ/ and /a/
- /o/ often as /ɔ/
- "r" often alveolar /r/
- "ou" as /u/
- "oi" as /wɛ/

### Cultural Context
- Strong French-Canadian identity
- Language laws (Bill 101) protecting French
- References to "maudit" weather, hockey, poutine
- Catholic influence in traditional expressions

---

## 🇧🇪 Belgian French

### Characteristics
- Mix of French and Walloon influence
- "tu" common but "vous" also used
- Distinctive expressions
- Warm, direct communication style

### Common Expressions
- "C'est bon ça" - That's good
- "Voilà" - used frequently at end of sentences
- "Quoi?" - often at end of statements
- "dis donc" - wow/hey
- "chelle" - cool (from "superbelle")

### Pronunciation
- /y/ (u sound) often as /i/
- Nasal vowels sometimes different
- Slight German influence in some areas
- "r" can be uvular or alveolar

### Cultural Context
- Influence from Dutch (Flemish)
- Beer culture references
- Comics tradition (Tintin, Spirou)
- "Les Schtroumpfs" references

---

## 🇨🇩 African French (Multiple Regions)

### West Africa (Senegal, Ivory Coast)

**Characteristics**
- French as official language with local variations
- Heavy use of French-based pidgins
- Direct communication style

**Common Expressions**
- "ça gaze" - what's up
- "yako" - let's go / come on
- "faire laoko" - to chat
- "wesh" - what's up (from France, adopted)
- "ça sent" - it's good

**Pronunciation**
- Faster pace
- More open vowels
- Sometimes dropped final consonants
- Tonal influence from local languages

**Cultural Context**
- Local language mix in informal speech
- French seen as "urban" language
- Griots/storytellers influence
- Arabic influence in some regions (Senegal)

### North Africa (Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia)

**Characteristics**
- Darija (dialect) mixing with French
- Heavy code-switching
- French for education/business

**Common Expressions (from Arabic)**
- "labas" - how are you
- "besh" - so that / in order to
- "wallah" - I swear (god)
- "khoya" - my brother
- "ya salam" - oh my god (surprise)

**Pronunciation**
- Arabic phonetic influence
- /q/ sometimes for /k/
- Emphasis on certain syllables
- Fast pace like Arabic

**Cultural Context**
- French as colonial inheritance
- Arabic/Tamazight influence on dialect
- Strong French education system
- Music and entertainment industry

---

## 🇸🇪 Swiss French

### Characteristics
- German influence on vocabulary
- "vous" common
- Distinct expressions from neighboring regions

### Common Expressions
- "Bon, alors" - well then (frequently)
- "ou alors" - or else
- "Ça va ?" - very common greeting

### Pronunciation
- Some /y/ as /i/
- German influence on intonation
- Clear enunciation

---

## 🇱🇦 Louisiana French

### Characteristics
- Historical French from 18th century
- Heavily influenced by English and Spanish
- Endangered language, revitalization efforts

### Common Expressions
- "cher" - dear (like French Canadian)
- "faire le grand chemin" - to travel
- "ça me fait grand" - I'm happy

### Pronunciation
- Older French pronunciation preserved
- "r" as alveolar /r/
- Some English phonetic influence

---

## 🇭🇹 Haitian Creole

Not French, but related:
- French-based creole
- Distinct grammar and vocabulary
- French speakers can understand ~40-60%
- Uses "ki" instead of "que", "sa" instead of "ça"
```

## Cultural Context Why It Matters

### For Comprehension
- Knowing "char" = "voiture" helps understand Quebec media
- Understanding "tabernacle" = mild swear helps with register
- Recognizing "yako" = "let's go" shows regional slang

### For Immersion
- Quebec podcasts, music, shows available
- African French influencers on social media
- Belgian comics and cartoons

### For Communication
- Different formality expectations
- "tu" vs "vous" varies by region
- Directness differs (Quebec more direct)

### For Identity
- Language = identity marker
- Dialect pride vs stigma
- Political dimensions (Quebec language laws)

3. **Studying region-specific materials**

4. **Understanding cultural references**

```markdown
- Uses "char", "magasiner"
- Pronounces "r" more alveolar
- Says "c'est quoi" often

## Cross-Dialect Interaction

- France speaker asks about Quebec words

- Discuss regional identity through language
- France speaker uses some Quebec expressions

- Good exposure to both dialects

- Cultural exchange through language
```
